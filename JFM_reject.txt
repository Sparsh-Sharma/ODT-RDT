Reviewer(s)' Comments to Author:

Referee: 1

Comments to the Author
The paper presents a strain-coupled extension of ODT for homogeneous turbulence under an imposed mean strain. The formulation itself is thoughtfully constructed and the implementation appears careful. I found the single-line closure developed later in the paper to be the most interesting aspect of the work, and the associated derivation seems, as far as I can see, correct. My difficulty is not with the mathematics but with the claims made on the basis of the resulting model. The manuscript demonstrates that the proposed formulation behaves in a plausible and internally consistent way, but I was not persuaded that the main physical conclusions have been validated to the extent required for a JFM paper. As discussed below, I think the work remains somewhat incomplete both as a study of strained turbulence and as a study motivated by leading-edge noise.

My major issues are the following:

1) The main result is not quantitatively validated outside the model itself. The central claim is that at Skt/ε ≈ 0.8 the spectrum develops a broadband distortion that differs from the rigid spectral translation predicted by rapid-distortion theory. This is established by comparing the model with itself, once with the strain switched on and once with it switched off. Since the departure from linear theory is generated by the modelled eddy dynamics, that comparison demonstrates the behaviour of the model rather than the behaviour of the flow. The manuscript itself notes the need for external validation. The comparison with Chen et al. remains qualitative, while the comparison with Lee & Reynolds contains no spectral information. As a result, the predicted distorted spectrum is never directly compared against an independent spectral benchmark.

2) The most original theoretical development is not actually used in the reported calculations. The simulations are performed using the closure introduced in Section 3.2, whereas the more complete single-line formulation developed later is not used in generating the principal results. I accept the argument that the moment closure represents the isotropic limit of the more general formulation, but that justification is strongest near isotropy. The calculations are carried to strains where the manuscript itself shows the closure becoming inaccurate. Consequently, it is unclear whether the main spectral conclusions would remain unchanged under the formulation that the paper ultimately argues is the more appropriate one.

3) The rapid pressure–strain closure appears to become qualitatively inaccurate over part of the strain range examined. Under plane strain, the exact solution predicts growth of the neutral spanwise component, which eventually overtakes the upwash component, whereas both closures predict the opposite trend. The manuscript acknowledges this discrepancy. What remains unclear is how sensitive the subsequent spectral predictions are to this known deficiency. Some assessment of that sensitivity seems necessary if the model is to be applied in this regime.

4) The connection to the leading-edge application is not yet carried through. First, it is not demonstrated that the spectrum produced by the ODT line is the same quantity required by Amiet’s theory, which is naturally formulated in terms of chordwise and spanwise wavenumbers. Second, the strain history responsible for generating the turbulence distortion is deferred to future work. Since the accumulated strain also determines the validity of the closure, the absence of a mapping between the model operating point and the actual stagnation-flow environment leaves an important gap in the proposed application.

As it stands, I do not think the work is sufficiently developed in either of the two directions it sets out to address. As a study of strained turbulence, the principal spectral prediction still requires quantitative validation against DNS or experiments. As a study motivated by leading-edge noise, the connection between the modelled distortion and the actual aeroacoustic problem remains incomplete.

Some further minor points:
- The introduction somewhat overstates the novelty of considering distortion of the incoming turbulence before leading-edge interaction. Relevant work by dos Santos et al., Ribeiro et al. and Piccolo et al. should be discussed.
- I was not convinced by the treatment of the dissipation estimate. The reported value is obtained as the residual of two larger terms, while the independent gradient-based estimate differs by up to 30%.
- The comparison with Lee & Reynolds relies on identifying the present strain parameter with twice the quantity used in their work. Since that mapping supports the anisotropy comparison, it deserves further discussion.
- I do not think Propositions 1 and 2 should be presented as major results. They read more naturally as consistency checks on the formulation.
- The manuscript could be shortened considerably. Section 2 is explicitly non-original, and a significant portion of the implementation detail could be relegated to supplementary material without loss of substance.

Personally, I found the analysis in Section 5 to be the strongest and most original part of the manuscript, and I would have liked to see the study built more directly around that result. Taken individually, some of the issues above might be addressable. Taken together, however, I do not think the manuscript currently meets the standard of validation and development that I would expect for publication in JFM. For that reason, I cannot recommend publication in its present form.

Referee: 3

Comments to the Author
Overall assessment
The manuscript develops a strain-coupled extension of ODT for rapidly distorted turbulence, including mean-strain production, rapid pressure–strain redistribution, and spectral deformation. The topic is potentially interesting, but I do not think the main theoretical claims are established.
Two issues are particularly serious. First, the manuscript treats rigid translation of the one-dimensional spectrum as an exact linear-RDT benchmark, although this is neither derived from the full RDT equations nor demonstrated by a full spectral RDT calculation. Second, the rapid pressure–strain formulation in Section 5 is presented as a closed single-line functional, although it still depends on unresolved two-dimensional spectral information.
These issues affect the central results of the paper. In my view, they require substantial reconstruction of the theory and re-analysis of the main results. I therefore recommend rejection and do not consider the manuscript suitable for publication in JFM in its present form.

Detailed comments:
1. The linear-RDT spectral benchmark is not justified
Section 4.2.1 uses the RDT wavevector relation dk2/dt = -A22 k2 to argue that the one-dimensional component spectra undergo a rigid translation in wavenumber and that their spectral centroids therefore follow a scaling law. This rigid-translation scaling is then used as the linear-RDT benchmark for much of the spectral analysis and forms the basis for one of the paper’s main conclusions.
However, this is not established theoretically. In Equation (2.14), the Fourier velocity amplitudes depend on the full three-dimensional wavevector.  The manuscript provides no derivation showing that the projected spectrum must undergo a rigid translation.
The tests in Section 4.1 do not establish this result either, since they verify the proposed ODT model only at the Reynolds-stress level, rather than at the spectral level. This directly affects the conclusions in Figures 4–10 concerning the departure from linear RDT and the claimed suppression of spectral migration.

2. The claimed single-line closure of the rapid pressure–strain term is not closed
Section 5 claims that axisymmetry reduces the exact 3D rapid pressure–strain integral to a closed functional of quantities available on a single ODT line. However, Eq. (5.7) still requires the two-variable spectral functions a(k2,kperp) and c(k2,kperp) and integration over the unresolved transverse wavenumber kperp. It does not show how these 2D functions can be recovered from the 1D spectra available on the ODT line.
This missing step is especially serious because Eqs. (5.7)–(5.10) are presented as the central result of Section 5 and as a “closed, computable functional.” In fact, the paper appears to derive only an axisymmetric 2D representation, not a single-line closure. Moreover, this spectral formulation is not used in the simulations, so its claimed computability and practical role in the model are not demonstrated.

3.The theoretical presentation is not sufficiently clear or self-contained
The manuscript frequently mixes established RDT results, existing closures, assumptions, and new developments without clearly separating them. In Section 5, “A1” is repeatedly used without formal definition, while Eqs. (5.8)–(5.10), described as a central result, appear essentially without derivation.

I have identified several additional issues, but given the more fundamental problems above, I do not think a detailed enumeration is necessary for the present recommendation.
