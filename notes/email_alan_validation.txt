Alan,

Glad the clock landed where we hoped. Two quick updates.

I ran the full noise chain against four published leading-edge
experiments. In the cleanest facilities we're within 1-2 dB of the
microphones and the interference lobes come out right. The computed
distortion does what it should - nothing on the thin plates, and it
pulls the NACA0012 toward the data. One caveat: at lab strains the far
field can't tell your clock from the standard model. The rigs just
never reach the strains where the mechanism works.

The spectra can tell them apart, though. On fresh 1024-realisation
ensembles the clock sits two to four times closer to exact RDT than
the baseline, and the error stops growing with strain - flat at 15-20%
out to e = 2, where the baseline drifts to 40%. That halves the
uncertainty we feed into the noise calculation.

The one setup that would show the difference on a microphone is a
turbulence-ingesting rotor. I've put that in the paper as the
falsifiable target rather than claim more than the data support.

Manuscript is now my full-time job - one paper, three parts. Draft to
you shortly.
