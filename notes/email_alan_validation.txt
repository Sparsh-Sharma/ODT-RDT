Glad the clock landed where we hoped. Two updates since this morning,
one of them yours.

I pushed the noise chain all the way to measurement: four published
leading-edge-noise experiments (Paterson & Amiet's NACA0012, the ECL
and ISVR flat plates), each predicted absolutely with the full
non-compact Amiet response and four inflows - frozen von Karman,
Liepmann, and our computed spectra with and without your clock. Where
the facilities are cleanest the chain lands within 1-2 dB rms of the
microphones with the interference lobes reproduced, and the computed
distortion behaves causally: it vanishes for the thin plates and moves
the NACA0012 predictions towards the data. Honest caveat: at
laboratory strains the radiated level cannot tell the standard model
from the clock - the rigs are designed to avoid the strains where the
mechanism acts, and the kernel barely weights the sub-cap scales.

The result that is yours: on fresh 1024-realisation ensembles at both
rapidities, the clock's strained spectra sit two to four times closer
to the exact-RDT family than the standard model's, and the distance no
longer grows with strain - it holds at 15-20% from e=0.5 to 2 while
the standard model degrades to 40%. Your conservation-constrained
stream, dose-matched, essentially removes the strain-dependence of the
model's spectral error. In the noise chain that halves the uncertainty
band on the input and makes it strain-independent.

The configuration that would discriminate the clock in a microphone
signal is a turbulence-ingesting rotor - streamtube contraction for
the strain, blade response for the fine-scale weighting. I have put
that in the paper as the falsifiable target rather than claiming more
than the data allow.

I am now working the manuscript full time - one paper, three parts
(closure, measured limits and the clock, noise consequence), with the
validation above closing part three. Draft to you shortly.
